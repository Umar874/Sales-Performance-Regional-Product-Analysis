# Sales-Performance-Regional-Product-Analysis
A Power BI dashboard analyzing $435K in Q1 grocery sales, breaking down performance by salesperson, region, product category, state, and customer. Includes a full technical report with individual salesperson deep dives, seasonal trends, and data-backed recommendations for closing performance gaps.

**Overview**

I analyzed $435,040 in Q1 grocery retail sales, tracking revenue by salesperson, region, state, product category, and customer. The result is a Power BI dashboard built around live KPIs, regional and monthly trend charts, and a sortable product table, backed by a full technical report covering every salesperson, region, and category individually.

**The Problem**

A single quarterly revenue total tells leadership almost nothing about where that money actually came from, or where it is at risk. Behind this business's healthy $435K Q1 sat a sales team with a sixfold performance gap between its top and bottom performer, a region generating nearly a third of all revenue on its own, and several salespersons whose entire book of business depended on a single customer. None of that shows up in a top-line number.

The dashboard combines Total Revenue and Quantity Purchased KPI cards with a regional lead callout, a state performance breakdown, a regional revenue share donut chart, a monthly revenue trend line, a salesperson revenue ranking, and a sortable product revenue table, filterable by salesperson, product, and region.

**What I Found**

Nancy Freehafer led all salespersons at $104,252.34 in revenue. Jan Kotas, at the bottom, generated $16,350.50, less than a sixth of the top performer's total. That gap alone would justify a closer look, but the data went further: several of the lowest-performing salespersons, including Jan Kotas, Andrew Cencini, Michael Neipper, and Robert Zare, were each relying on just one or two customers for their entire revenue. That is not just a performance gap. It is a concentration risk sitting quietly underneath it.

Regional performance told a similarly uneven story. The North generated $141,680.34, 32.56% of total revenue, well ahead of East, South, and West, which clustered much closer together between roughly 21% and 25% each. Beverages emerged as the business's clear anchor category at $110,577.11, more than the next two categories combined, making it the single highest-risk item to ever run short on shelf.

The monthly trend added a layer of seasonality that repeated itself almost everywhere I looked. December and June stood out consistently as the strongest months, not just company-wide but within individual salesperson performance too, while February was the weakest month across nearly every cut of the data. That kind of consistency is not coincidence, it is a specific, investigable pattern.

**Why It Matters**

None of these findings required more data than what was already sitting in the dataset. They required comparing salesperson against region, region against product category, and month against month, until the patterns that a single revenue total would have hidden started to surface on their own.
